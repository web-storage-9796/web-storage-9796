Types of smart lock

Motorised retrofit lock. The easiest type to fit: it sits on the inside of the door, over the cylinder, and turns the key left in the lock on that side. Nothing changes on the outside, and it can be removed when you move out, which is handy if you rent.

Full electronic lock. Replaces the lock or handle set and adds a keypad, fingerprint or card reader on the outside. The door's measurements need checking, and sometimes the cut-outs have to be adapted.

Locks for holiday lets and rentals. These can be either type, but let you create codes or access with a start and end date and time, and cancel them from the app.

No single model is best for everyone. It depends on the door, how you use it and whether other people need access. That is why we do not recommend a particular brand without seeing the door first.

Security doors and lock cylinders

The lock cylinder matters most. A motorised retrofit lock turns a key left in the lock on the inside, so many models need a double-clutch cylinder or one with an emergency function. Without it, you will not be able to open the door from outside with your key while the smart lock is fitted.

On security doors we also check the cylinder length, how far it sticks out on the inside and the space around it: the handle, knob or security shield can get in the way of the device. We check all this from photos before you buy anything and, if needed, swap the cylinder for a compatible one.

Security: what a smart lock does not do

A smart lock adds convenience and access control, but it does not make the door any stronger. Fitted over an old cylinder, the door is just as exposed to lock bumping, picking or snapping as before.

The foundation should be an anti-bump security cylinder protected on the outside by a security shield. On top of that, a smart lock adds convenience without taking away security.

Battery. Most models warn you in the app when the battery is running low. If it runs out, a motorised retrofit lock can still be opened from outside with the normal key, as long as the cylinder has an emergency function; keypad locks usually have a mechanical key or a socket to power them from outside.
