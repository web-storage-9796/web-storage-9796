What helps us assess the emergency

Tell us whether the door has slammed shut or is locked with a key, whether a key has snapped, whether the mechanism turns and whether there's any visible damage. A WhatsApp photo of the lock and the edge of the door helps us identify the system. Your location and the type of lock affect both arrival time and price.

The most common emergencies and how we handle them

Each emergency calls for a different approach. These are the ones we see most and what we usually do:

  • Door slammed shut: if only the latch has caught, it can usually be opened without damaging the lock.
  • Door locked with a key or deadbolt: we assess the cylinder and, if there's no other option, replace it when we finish.
  • Key broken inside the cylinder: we extract the piece and check whether the cylinder has been damaged.
  • Lost or stolen key: we open the door and recommend changing the cylinder, because someone may have your key.
  • Lock forced after a break-in: we secure the entrance so the home or shop isn't left open.

While we're on our way

A few minutes of preparation save time and avoid damage:

  • If a child, someone who needs help or a lit stove is inside, call 112 first.
  • Don't try to force the lock with cards or screwdrivers: you can damage the cylinder and make the opening harder.
  • If you suspect a break-in, don't go inside and call the police before calling us.
  • Have a document ready that shows you live there or have permission to enter.
