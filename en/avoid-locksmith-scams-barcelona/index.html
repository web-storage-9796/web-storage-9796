How to avoid locksmith scams in Barcelona

When you are locked out you are in a hurry, and that is when people get overcharged. A few questions on the phone are enough to avoid most problems.

Be wary of bait prices

"From €49" is hardly ever what you pay: it usually leaves out the part, VAT and the call-out.

Ask before they come

Ask on the phone for the total price including VAT for your situation, and what happens if you turn down the quote.

Insist on a traceable company

Company name, tax number (NIF), address and a VAT invoice. Without them, complaining later is much harder.

Common rip-offs

Most locksmiths are honest, but the urgency of being locked out makes it easy to take advantage. In 2025 the Spanish consumer organisation OCU reported door openings charged at over €400, around four times the average price, according to its press release (in Spanish). These are the tricks that come up most often:

  • Bait prices: "from €49" or "from €59" that leaves out the part, VAT and the call-out.
  • Price only on arrival: no figure on the phone, then the amount shoots up once they are at your door.
  • Call centres posing as a "local" locksmith: websites named after your area that pass the job to whoever is free.
  • A whole new lock when a cylinder would do: often only the cylinder needs replacing.
  • Vague about VAT: a price that turns out to exclude VAT, or a discount for paying without an invoice.
  • Cash only or no invoice: with no receipt you have nothing to complain with.
  • High charges for turning down the quote: a "visit fee" nobody mentioned that costs almost as much as the job.

What to ask on the phone before they come

A two-minute call avoids most surprises. Explain whether the door slammed shut or is locked, and whether it is a reinforced door, then ask:

  • What is the total price including VAT for my situation?
  • Does it include the call-out and parts?
  • If I turn down the quote when you arrive, do I have to pay anything? How much?
  • What is the company called? Does it have a tax number and an address?
  • Will you give me a VAT invoice? Can I pay by card?
  • How long will you take to get here?

At the door, before they start

Ask them to confirm the price before touching the lock. If it differs from what you were told on the phone, ask why and which part is needed.

If they suggest replacing the whole lock, ask whether a new cylinder would do. On many doors it would, and the price difference is large: see our lock change page.

Being asked for ID, a tenancy agreement or a utility bill before they open the door is a good sign: a reputable locksmith checks that you live there.

What to do if you have already been overcharged

Even if you have already paid, you can still complain. Follow these steps:

  • Ask for an itemised invoice showing each item, its price, VAT, and the company's tax number and address. Keep photos of the parts, your proof of payment and any messages.
  • Ask for the official complaint form. In Catalonia it is called the "full oficial de queixa, reclamació o denúncia", and businesses serving consumers must provide it.
  • Complain to the company first using that form and wait for its reply.
  • If they do not reply or the answer is not good enough, go to the Catalan Consumer Agency (Agència Catalana del Consum), which handles consumer complaints in Catalonia.
  • You can also check the OCU, which publishes warnings and advice for consumers.
