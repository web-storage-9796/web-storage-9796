How to make your home more secure against burglary

Good protection works in layers. We check the door, frame, cylinder, shield and other entry points to prioritise the improvements that suit your home. No single device can guarantee that a burglary won't happen, but a well-matched set of measures increases resistance and reduces weak points.

Door and frame

We check the leaf, frame, hinges, gaps and locking points. An upgraded lock needs a solid base that can withstand force.

Cylinder and shield

We check the cylinder, how far it sticks out, who controls the keys and the external protection, to suggest compatible improvements.

Other entry points

Windows, balconies, courtyards, garages, storage rooms and shared entrances are also part of a home security check.

Home security checklist

The check starts with the real entry points and how you use them every day. It's not about installing everything, but finding the weakest point and prioritising the right measures.

  • Condition of the door, frame, hinges and fixings.
  • Type of lock, cylinder, shield and number of locking points.
  • Who has copies of the keys, and what to do if keys are lost or stolen.
  • Protection for windows, courtyards, terraces and shared entrances.
  • Lighting, visibility and locking habits when you go out.

How to reinforce a door without necessarily replacing it

The front door is the entry point burglars try most often, so it's worth reinforcing it as a whole. Depending on its condition and compatibility, options include a high-security cylinder, a security shield, a multipoint lock or frame reinforcement. If the leaf or structure has serious limitations, a security door may make more sense. The decision should consider the whole door, not just one part.

Measures for ground floors, top-floor flats and homes with courtyards

Openings that can be reached from outside need their own check. Depending on the window, the escape route and the building's rules, options include bars, opening grilles, extra locks or sensors. In garages and shared buildings, it's also worth checking that doors close properly and that keys or remotes are collected from anyone no longer authorised.

What to do if you see signs of tampering or a burglary

New marks, parts that have moved or a lock that behaves oddly don't by themselves prove an attempted burglary. If you find the door open or forced, don't go in, don't touch anything, move away and call 112. Follow police instructions before recording damage or arranging a repair. The entrance can then be secured temporarily and a permanent solution assessed.

Official security sources

For official advice, see the Mossos d'Esquadra's home security tips and the Government of Catalonia's guide on burglary and theft. In an emergency, call 112.
