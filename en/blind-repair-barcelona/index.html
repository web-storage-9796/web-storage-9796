Diagnosis before repairing a blind

A blind that won't go up, comes down crooked or makes noise can have several causes. We check the mechanism and its parts to decide whether a specific part needs adjusting, repairing or replacing. That way we avoid changing parts that still work. Repairing the right part avoids unnecessary costs.

Shop shutters: security and reliability

In a shop, a shutter in poor condition can affect access and day-to-day trading. After inspecting it, we tell you what work is needed and the options for getting it working safely again, so your business can open and close with peace of mind.

Common breakdowns in manual and electric blinds

A worn strap or strap winder, displaced slats, a damaged axle or loose guides can stop a blind moving. In motorised blinds, we also check the power supply, the switch or remote, the motor and the limit switches. The diagnosis helps you decide between adjusting, replacing a part, motorising or replacing the whole blind when repair is no longer worthwhile.

Broken strap or a winder that won't wind

This is the most common fault on manual roller shutters. If the strap snaps with the shutter up, don't let it drop: lower it by hand carefully or hold it in place so it doesn't fall and damage the slats.

Replacing the strap means opening the shutter box above the window and fitting the new strap to the winder as well. If the winder's spring has gone and it no longer rewinds, the whole winder is replaced. We check the axle and pulley at the same time so the new strap doesn't wear out early.

Slipped slats or a crooked shutter

When a shutter comes down crooked or sticks halfway, slats have usually slipped out of the guide rail, from a gust of wind or from being forced, or have broken. Individual slats can be replaced if the same profile is available, without replacing the whole shutter.

If the shutter has dropped completely, the straps or clips holding it to the axle have usually failed. We also check the bottom slat and the stops that keep the shutter from disappearing into the box.

Motorised shutter not responding

Before replacing a motor, we check the switch or remote and the power supply. If the motor stops after several ups and downs in a row, it may be the thermal cut-out, which pauses it for a few minutes to stop it overheating. If the shutter doesn't stop at the top or bottom, the limit settings need adjusting. For metal shop shutters, see persianas.merafex.es.
