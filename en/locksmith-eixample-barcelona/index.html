Two doors to check: your flat's and the building's

Eixample was built between the 19th and early 20th centuries on Ildefons Cerdà's grid, with chamfered-corner blocks and multi-storey buildings sharing a single street entrance. Many buildings mix homes, offices and shops, so the shared entrance is used heavily. That is why many problems involve two levels: the door of the flat and the building entrance.

Old street doors: repair before replacing

Some buildings keep their original wooden or iron street door. When it stops closing properly, the cause is usually the lock, the closer or the electric strike rather than the door itself, and we check those first. If the building is listed in the City Council's heritage catalogue, any visible change to the façade may need permission, so the owners' association should check before deciding.   • Keyed-alike or master key systems for the entrance, garage and other shared doors.

Rented and holiday flats: keeping control of the keys

Ciutat Vella and Eixample account for a large share of Barcelona's licensed holiday flats, and the district also has many long-term rentals. When tenants or guests come and go often, it is easy to lose track of copies. After a move or a change of manager, replacing the cylinder is the most direct way to invalidate old copies. A cylinder with an ownership card, where the model allows it, restricts new copies to whoever holds the card. See lock change.

What to tell us when calling from Eixample

On the grid, every junction has four chamfered corners. These details help us find the right entrance and prepare the job:

  • Street and number and, if the building is on a corner, which street the entrance is on.
  • Whether the problem is in the flat, the street door or a ground-floor shop.
  • Whether there is a concierge and their hours, or whether someone needs to come down to let us in.
  • A photo of the lock and the door edge on WhatsApp, if you can take one.
